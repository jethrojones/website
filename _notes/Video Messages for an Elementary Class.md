A useful class video message is short, clear, and has one purpose. Decide what students should notice, feel, or do before you press record.

It does not need polished production. A quiet room, a steady camera, and a direct message are enough when students can hear and understand you.

<!--more-->

## Seven Useful Video Messages for a Grade-School Class

- **Morning welcome:** greet students, name the day's focus, and give them one thing to listen for.
- **Absent-teacher message:** explain the day's plan, where to begin, and how students can record questions for their teacher.
- **Guest introduction:** introduce a guest or a topic so students know why the message matters before they watch.
- **Encouragement before hard work:** remind students to choose a first step, ask for help, and keep going.
- **Celebration:** recognize a completed project, a helpful choice, or progress toward a class goal.
- **Family or community greeting:** invite a family or community member to share a brief welcome connected to classroom learning.
- **End-of-year message:** reflect on what the class learned and encourage students as they move to their next step.

## Five Short Scripts

### Morning Welcome

Good morning, [class name]. Today we are learning about [topic]. Listen for one idea you want to remember, then bring that idea to class.

### Encouragement Before Hard Work

Before you start [task], choose one small first step. You may not know the whole answer yet, and that is okay. Work carefully, ask for help, and keep going.

### When the Teacher Is Away

Hello, [class name]. Your teacher is away today, and the plan for [topic] is ready. Follow the directions, work respectfully, and write down one question to discuss when your teacher returns.

### Guest or Community Message

Hello, [class name]. This message is about [topic]. As you watch, think about one way the topic connects to your classroom, family, or community.

### Class Celebration

Hello, [class name]. Your class has reached [goal] after working on [project]. Notice one choice that helped, thank someone who contributed, and choose one next step.

## Keep the Video Safe and Easy to Share

- [ ] Record and share the video through a school-approved account or channel.
- [ ] Show student names or faces only when the required permission is in place.
- [ ] Leave out schedules, locations, and personal details.
- [ ] Check the background for private papers, screens, names, and other identifying information.
- [ ] Add captions before sharing.
- [ ] Preview the final link and the surrounding comments and suggested content.
- [ ] Follow school/district policy for recording, posting, and student privacy.

## Give Students Something to Do After the Video

Before the video, give students one listening prompt: ask them to listen for the one idea they can use today. After the video, ask for one response that names the idea and a next step, in writing, drawing, or a short discussion.

Sample questions:

- What is one idea from the video that you want to remember?
- How does the message connect to [topic] or something we are doing in class?
- What is one action you can take after watching?
