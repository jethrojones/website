A couple years ago, I hired a VA.

Not one in the Philippines or somewhere overseas. She lived in Nashville. She was supporting her family, and she was really good.

I remember asking her, “I’ve never had a VA before. What’s the most valuable thing you can do for me?”

She said, “I’m going to organize your inbox and put everything where it needs to go.”

I remember thinking, *That sounds so stupid.*

<!--more-->

I couldn’t imagine how that was actually going to help me.

We talked some more, found some other things for her to do, and she ended up being really helpful.

But here’s what’s crazy…

I was thinking about it today, and I realized that basically everything she used to do, I now do with OpenClaw and Hermes.

Actually, that’s not even true.

OpenClaw and Hermes do **more** than I ever had her doing.

And they do it instantly.

The other thing I hadn’t thought about back then is that she wasn’t available whenever I needed her. If I had a meeting coming up in 20 minutes and I needed something done, I’d usually just do it myself because I didn’t want to wait.

AI doesn’t have that problem.

It’s just…there.

Twenty-four hours a day.

And that’s what got me thinking.

First, what was *I* thinking? I completely underestimated how valuable it was to have someone taking those little tasks off my plate.

But second…

What are VA companies thinking?

Seriously.

How is that business model sustainable if the value you’re providing is organizing inboxes, moving files around, drafting emails, scheduling appointments, and all the other administrative stuff?

AI is just better at that now.

And it’s only getting better.

I’m not saying people don’t matter anymore. They absolutely do.

But I do think the value has shifted.

The person who’s valuable today isn’t the one clicking buttons all day. It’s the one who knows how to build systems, use AI well, catch mistakes, and solve the problems AI can’t solve.

That’s a completely different job.

It’s just wild to me that something I thought sounded like a dumb service a couple years ago is now one of the most valuable things in my workflow…

…except it’s software doing it instead of a person.
