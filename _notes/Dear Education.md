Dear Education,

You've had a good run. You've provided education, stability, babysitting, and extra-curriculars for millions of kids. 

But the world is changing in ways that even you have to adapt. 

You have the potential to make something amazing happen that is unprecedented. 

You have the ability to help kids be ready for a future that they (and you) can't possibly comprehend. And that's an inspiring place to be. 

First, of course, kids need to be able to read, write, and do arithmetic. 

But they need to be able to do so much more. 

I propose a slightly different approach. 

This will be hard, but it is so much more fun. 

Instead of starting with the standards, end with them. 

What I mean is, instead of starting with the standards and trying to teach them to kids, start with activities, events, competitions, projects, and follow the kids and attach standards to what they accomplish. 

Humans are naturally curious, and love to solve hard problems. Let kids do that, and instead of making fake problems for them to solve, challenge them to solve real problems. You will see their ability to learn through that process. 

That will require school to be structured differently. 

Some things that we thought were important may not be classes anymore. But on the contrary, there are several "classes" that we never could have possibly conceived that could be created. 

But classes or subjects are the wrong words. 

Let me give an example. In my school, way back in 2017, we did this. We called it Synergy, and you can find out more about it by searching for synergy at [TransformativePrincipal.org](https://transformativeprincipal.org/search?query=synergy). 

Synergy was a portion of our day (2 90 minute sessions each week) where kids were challenged to solve a problem that would make the world a better place. It was intentionally outside the regular subjects. Every adult in the building was involved. Kids learned to do all kinds of amazing things. Student to adult ratio dropped to 13:1. 

But here's the best part. We followed one group and checked off the standards they accomplished by doing their project. They showed mastery of over forty standards, extending all the way up to 12th grade level standards. 

So, not only did they pass off many in their current grade level of 7th or 8th grade, they also showed mastery of levels much higher than we were prepared to teach (but we were prepared to assess at that higher level). In fact, they even learned things that we don't teach in school! 

Here's the amazing part: if we had tried to teach all those skills to them in the traditional way, it would have taken us at leaast 150 direct instruction lessons in 10 different subjects, and they would have been super boring lessons for everyone involved, teachers and students included.

There are a few things to be aware of. Not every kid will learn the exact same things at the exact same time. 

That's OK! 

Not every kid will learn the exact same things as their neighbor. 

That's OK, too! 

And you know what? That's the current reality also! 

Kids already don't learn the same things as their neighbor, and then we put them in school more and tell them they are dumb because they didn't get it the first time. 

Kids with natural proclivities to certain subjects will do more of that thing they are interested in. Again, that's OK! 

But here's the important thing: If kids know how to learn something new without having to get direct instruction from a teacher, they will be prepared to learn whatever thing they need to learn at the moment they need to learn it. 

Yes, learning will still take time. That's OK, too! 

But, kids who are confident in their ability to learn something new when they need to learn won't be afraid of changing times and seasons and instead will have confidence to go about their lives ready to learn whatever the world throws at them. 

Here's a very real scenario: someone is not going to get to Algebra in High School. That's OK, too! When they need it, they will learn it, because they have learned how to learn what they need to accomplish something. Some things they may never learn. That's ok, too!  

That's not to say that teachers will be out of a job. But teachers will need to shift from being the [sage on the stage to the compass among us](https://jethrojones.com/compass). In fact, this is likely what most teachers love the most. They get to be there for all the lightbulb moments that they crave, because they happen much more often in this scenario. 

There will still be a need for students to learn something specifically, for which starting with the standard is a great tool. But the difference is that kids don't have to learn that from sitting in a class with 30 other kids at varying levels. 

Teachers can make a great impact by teaching exactly what a kid needs right when he or she needs it. How powerful to be the one who gets to help a kid pass a huge hurdle because of what you can teach them! You know you love that feeling! 

And, the great thing is you can start this today in your schools. This is exactly what I would do if I were a superintendent today: 

1. Challenge each teacher to change one assignment from starting with standards to ending with standards. 
2.  Designate time outside of classes but in the school day for project-focused work by partnering with businesses, community members, nonprofits, churches, and my own district office, to find real problems that need solutions.
3. Give kids and teachers who show they "get it" to start opting out of the traditional structures, so there is choice within the buildings.
4. Make a 2 year plan to get everyone there. 

Yes, change is hard. But do you know what is harder? Forced change when you are behind the eight ball. 

I know these ideas are considered radical, but they do work. 

We were doing this years ago with much less advanced tools, and when COVID hit, our school barely missed a beat because we already had taught our students how to learn, and things went incredibly smoothly. 

My plea to you, education, is to seize the moment and be a leader. Don't waste your time on AI policies (nobody reads them anyway). Don't waste your time on holding on to how we've always done things. 

Seize the opportunity that is in front of you and provide the personalized, focused education that every kid deserves, the kind of education that will help that student live a fulfilled life. 

Love, 

Jethro
